Singapore Core Financial Regulatory Laws Notes (MAS/PDPA/FSMA)
I. Core Rules of MAS (Monetary Authority of Singapore)
1. Core Positioning
MAS is not only a regulatory authority but also a rule-maker and risk controller in Singapore's financial industry. Its core logic is to prevent risks, promote compliance and maintain stability. All rules ultimately focus on three goals: anti-money laundering, countering the financing of terrorism and protecting consumer rights and interests.
2. High-Frequency Practical Points 
MAS 626: The most frequently tested and core regulation focusing on anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism. Focus on three key thresholds:
        
Daily cash transactions for individuals ≥ SGD 20,000 require mandatory recording and retention.
Daily cross-border transactions for individuals ≥ SGD 15,000 trigger the Travel Rule, requiring reporting of both parties' information.
Cumulative daily transactions for enterprises ≥ SGD 100,000 require secondary compliance review.
Suspicious Transactions: Focus on abnormality instead of memorizing all clauses. Structured transactions, rapid inflow and outflow of funds, sudden changes in long-dormant accounts, counterparties from high-risk countries and missing UBO information are key identification logics.
KYC/CDD/EDD Requirements: Conduct CDD for ordinary customers and EDD for high-risk customers. The core is to verify identity, confirm source of funds and conduct UBO penetration checks.
3. Practical Insights
First, the rigidity of MAS rules: Thresholds cannot be adjusted arbitrarily and should be hard-coded in the project to reflect compliance rigor. Second, risk priority in practice: Transactions with abnormal characteristics should be marked as medium or high risk even if the amount does not reach the threshold, which is the core thinking of Singapore's risk control. Third, alignment with FATF 40 Recommendations: As a high-compliance country, Singapore's MAS 626 is fully aligned with FATF requirements.
II. PDPA (Personal Data Protection Act)
1. Core Positioning
PDPA is not an additional compliance requirement but a bottom-line rule for the financial industry. Financial institutions hold a large amount of sensitive customer data. Violations of PDPA will not only result in fines but also affect the institution's license. Therefore, PDPA verification logic must be embedded in projects.
2. High-Frequency Practical Points 
Core Principles: Data Minimization and Consent. Only collect customer data necessary for projects or business and obtain explicit consent before collection.
Data Retention Period: The maximum retention period for financial data is 5 years, and a data retention reminder should be added in the project to avoid violations.
Sensitive Data Protection: Passport numbers, NRIC, transaction records and PEP status are sensitive data, which should be encrypted and not displayed in plain text.
Compliance Red Lines: Do not disclose or sell customer data at will, and do not use data for unintended purposes. Customers have the right to request deletion or modification of their personal data.
3. Practical Insights
First, PDPA implementation in projects: Focus on three key points without complex development: consent pop-up, data retention reminder and sensitive data encryption prompt. Second, linkage with financial compliance: PDPA is closely bound with KYC, and the collection of customer identity information must meet both KYC and PDPA requirements. Third, the synergy between PDPA and MAS: The 5-year data retention period is required by both PDPA and MAS, reflecting an overall understanding of the regulatory system.
III. FSMA (Financial Services and Markets Act 2022)
1. Core Positioning
FSMA is the fundamental law of Singapore's financial industry, covering all financial services. Its core is to regulate financial market order, protect investors and consumers, and prevent systemic risks. It provides a legal basis for MAS supervision and serves as the top-level support for project compliance.
2. High-Frequency Practical Points
License Management: Any institution engaged in financial services must obtain the corresponding license issued by MAS. The project should clearly state non-licensed simulation to avoid implied violations.
Compliance Obligations: Financial institutions must establish a sound compliance system and risk management system, and conduct regular compliance self-inspections.
Consumer Protection: Misleading publicity and vicious competition are prohibited, and risks must be fully disclosed to customers.
Linkage with PSA: PSA is a supporting regulation of FSMA focusing on payment institution supervision. Transactions and payment-related functions in the project must align with both FSMA and PSA requirements.
3. Practical Insights
First, the umbrella role of FSMA: All rules in the project must be implemented within the framework of FSMA. Second, practical focus: Focus on license compliance and risk disclosure, and clearly state non-commercial and non-licensed nature in the project to avoid compliance risks. Third, relevance to the workplace: The core work of Singapore's risk control and compliance positions is to ensure that institutional operations comply with FSMA and supporting regulations.
IV. Synergy Logic of the Three Regulations 
FSMA provides the top-level legal framework; MAS is responsible for specific regulatory implementation; PDPA is the bottom line for data compliance. The three are closely linked and indispensable. Meeting the requirements of the three regulations in project development reflects comprehensive compliance, which is the core compliance logic of Singapore's financial institutions.
V. General Supplementary Insights
1. The core of all regulatory implementation is executability and verifiability. Convert rules into specific functions in the project and explain regulations in combination with functions for greater persuasiveness.
2. The core thinking of Singapore's supervision is prevention first. KYC due diligence and transaction monitoring are designed to prevent risks in advance rather than remedy them afterward.
3. Memory skills: Focus on thresholds, red lines and core principles, and understand them in combination with project scenarios to meet workplace needs.
